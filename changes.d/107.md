For `CLAUDE.md`, "Writing a post", after the LinkedIn draft paragraph (blog#107):

- A post can also have a LinkedIn Article: `docs/linkedin/<slug>-article.md`,
  with its cover `<slug>-cover.png`. Then `<slug>.md` is the Article's share
  text, not a link post. `tests/linkedin.mjs` holds the rules, from the
  research in blog#107's PR. The Article: a `# ` headline of 60 characters at
  most (LinkedIn cuts the search title there), "AI" and "edited" in the
  body's first 160 characters (the search preview), a sub-head at least every
  300 words, 3 sentences a paragraph at most (each list item counts alone),
  1,000 to 2,500 words, a question as the last paragraph, absolute links only,
  no em dash, and every number also in the post's title, description or body.
  The cover is a PNG at 1920 x 1080 (LinkedIn Help). The share text: 80 words
  at most, no URL (LinkedIn attaches the Article), "AI" first, 3 hashtags at
  most, no em dash. `ARTICLES` in `tests/linkedin.test.mjs` lists the
  Articles that must exist.
- `node scripts/linkedin-cover.mjs <slug>` draws the cover from the Article's
  headline, in the post's OG-image design. Run it again after the headline
  changes. It needs `node_modules`, so a worker runs it in its scratchpad copy
  and copies the PNG back.
- LinkedIn's editor does not read Markdown: the headline goes in the title
  field, and each `## ` line is made a heading by hand.

For `CLAUDE.md`, "Building from a worktree" (blog#107):

- In the scratchpad copy, run prettier as `./node_modules/.bin/prettier
  --write <files>`. `npx --no prettier --write` hands `--write` to npm as its
  own config, and prettier prints the files instead of writing them.
