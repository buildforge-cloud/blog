For `CLAUDE.md`, "Writing a post", after the LinkedIn draft paragraph (blog#107):

- A post can also have a LinkedIn Article: `docs/linkedin/<slug>-article.md`,
  with its cover `<slug>-cover.png`. Then `<slug>.md` is the Article's share
  text, not a link post. `tests/linkedin.mjs` holds the rules, from the
  research in blog#107's PR. An Article is new writing for LinkedIn, not a
  cut of the post (Stefan, decision 4408): at most 5 of its sentences are the
  post's word for word, and none of its sub-heads is one of the post's. It
  opens with a hook of 140 characters at most (whole before "see more" on a
  phone), then a short AI line of 40 at most that says "AI" and "edited"
  ("Written by AI, edited by me."), both within the first 160 characters
  (the search preview). Also: a `# ` headline of 60 characters at most
  (LinkedIn cuts the search title there), a sub-head at least every 300
  words, 3 sentences a paragraph at most (each list item counts alone), 1,000
  to 2,500 words, a question as the last paragraph, absolute links only, no
  em dash, and every number also in the post's title, description or body.
  The number check reads digits only ("ten" is not checked), skips a link's
  address, and reads 1,000 as 1000; a number the post has anywhere passes, so
  it catches a new number, not a wrong one. The cover is a PNG at 1920 x 1080
  (LinkedIn Help) whose iTXt "Title" is the Article's headline, so a cover
  left from an older headline fails. The share text: the same hook and AI
  line first, 80 words at most, no URL (LinkedIn attaches the Article), 3
  hashtags at most, no em dash. `ARTICLES` in `tests/linkedin.test.mjs` lists
  the Articles that must exist.
- `node scripts/linkedin-cover.mjs <slug>` draws the cover from the Article's
  headline, in the post's OG-image design with larger type (a 148 px
  headline, a 60 px byline, readable on a phone), and writes the headline
  into it with `scripts/png-title.mjs`. Run it again after the headline
  changes (the test fails until then). It finds its files from its own path,
  so it runs from any folder, but it needs `node_modules`: a worker runs the
  copy's script in its scratchpad copy and copies the PNG back.
- LinkedIn's editor does not read Markdown: the headline goes in the title
  field, and each `## ` line is made a heading by hand.

- In the scratchpad copy, run prettier as `./node_modules/.bin/prettier
  --write <files>`. `npx --no prettier --write` hands `--write` to npm as its
