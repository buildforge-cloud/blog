At my old job I worked on ISO 27001 certification. I found the process more
confusing than anything else.

So I built [ISO Pathfinder Buddy](https://iso-pathfinder-buddy.lovable.app/),
an ISO 27001 guide that runs in the browser. I wanted something light, that
helps a user along the way one step at a time. I also had a reason of my own.
I hoped that if I had to build something that explains ISO 27001 to others, I
would finally understand it myself.

## A first version on the first evening

I built it with [Lovable](https://lovable.dev) in January 2026. That was very
early for me. I had little experience coding with AI.

The speed surprised me. Lovable had a first version running the same evening
I started the project. It kept growing from there, through January and into
February. Today it has a setup wizard, a
[roadmap](https://iso-pathfinder-buddy.lovable.app/roadmap) in ten phases,
evidence templates, the Annex A controls, a glossary, and the upkeep that
comes after a certificate. There is no account. What a user enters stays in
their own browser.

Nothing went wrong on the way.

## I couldn't approve what I didn't understand

What took time was my part. Lovable could build a page about a phase or a
control in minutes. Before I said yes to it, I had to know it was right. And
I couldn't judge one piece without knowing where it sat among the others.

So I went through the descriptions and the support documents again and
again. Each time a new page came back, I had to work out how that part
connected to the rest. I hadn't planned to study the standard that way.
Having to approve each page forced me to.

It worked. I understand how ISO 27001 hangs together better now than I did
at my old job. I learned it while checking what the AI wrote, one page
at a time.

The dates:

- 10 January 2026: the project starts in Lovable, with a first version that
  evening.
- 24 February 2026: the last change made in Lovable.
- 10 November 2026: the app moves out of Lovable, into a repo of its own.

In November I moved it out of Lovable. Since then, the same AI agents that
work on my other apps work on this one too. One of their first jobs there was a routine update, and it turned up
[a hole in my secret scanner](/posts/secret-scanner-skipped-files-that-looked-like-archives/).
