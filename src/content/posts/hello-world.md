I got into building with AI because it closed the gap between an idea and
something I could actually look at, instead of just something that ran
underneath. That gap used to stop me cold.

I can't stand building something that only works. I need to see how it could
look, whether that's a mockup or just a rough skeleton, and I always struggled
to get there myself. I hate developing interfaces. Every idea I had would stall
right there: technically buildable, but stuck as something with no shape I
could actually react to.

AI changed that. I could watch an idea come to life instead of it staying stuck
in my head. So I decided to just build whatever I wanted, but stay close to the
developer side of things: I set up the infrastructure myself, think through
the architecture myself, and rely on Claude for the actual implementation.

So far that's turned into a batch of different things: a budget tracker, my
own homepage, a couple of invoice scanners, MCP servers that let me talk to
bigger systems in natural language, a project to turn an old low-code app into
something built on a real framework, a PS5 game database, a cycle tracker, a
tool for tailoring CVs. Different problems, different shapes, same approach
underneath.

The part I'm still working out isn't the building, it's the directing.
Figuring out how to actually communicate with the AI well, what checks and
balances I need before I trust an output, which skills I need to build up on
my own side, what I need to remember to check every time instead of assuming
it's handled. That list keeps growing, not shrinking.

This is where I'm going to write that down as it happens: what worked, what
didn't, and whatever I figure out about doing this well along the way.
