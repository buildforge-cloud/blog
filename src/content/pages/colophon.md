## The apps

Everything here is built with an AI agent doing the actual implementation, but
"I direct it and review the output" undersells how involved I actually am. I keep
a constant watch: I don't let it loose entirely. I trust its judgment on a lot of
things, but I usually have a pretty set mind about what I actually want done, and
I'm steering it toward that at any given point, not just approving after the
fact. I'll also braindump ideas mid-session while it's working, and it has to
pivot and reflect that in what it's doing. Posts document that loop as it
actually happened: the redirections and the places my instructions were wrong,
as much as the places the agent's output was.

## The writing

Same idea applies here, with a line I try to hold. AI can help me structure
notes, catch errors, and organize a draft, but the actual reflections and
opinions need to be mine. That's where the line sits for now. If it turns out to
be the wrong place to draw it as I write more posts, I'll move it and say so.

## What's not here

I don't publish raw session transcripts or link to the private repos most of this
runs on. Some of that's genuinely about security: there's real credentials and
other people's data running through that infrastructure. But it's not only that.
Sometimes I just don't think something's ready to publish, or I'd rather keep the
IP private, and that's reason enough on its own.
